Camera Calibration

The box camera uses a wide-angle lens, which distorts the image: straight lines look curved, especially near the edges, and a centimetre does not cover the same number of pixels in the centre of the image as in a corner. This panel measures that distortion with a printed grid of dots, so that you can correct it in your analyses, for example before converting the tracked positions of the animal into real distances. It also measures how tilted the camera is with respect to a surface, which helps to align it.

Always available in the CALIBRATION screen. It calibrates the box camera; the images of any other camera can be calibrated by loading them as files.

How It Works

The grid is a sheet of black dots at a known, exact spacing. The calibration compares where the dots really are (a perfect grid) with where the camera sees them, and works out the lens model that explains the difference: the camera matrix (focal length and optical centre) and the distortion coefficients. It uses the standard OpenCV calibration (calibrateCamera), adapted to a grid of circles.

A single image is not enough, because the tilt of the sheet (perspective) and the lens distortion look alike. With several images of the grid at different angles and positions, the two can be separated. Dots are used instead of the classic chessboard because the centre of a circle can be located with sub-pixel precision even in a slightly blurred image. The white crosses inside the dots and the grey lines between them are not used by the detection: they help to measure the sheet with a ruler and to align several sheets.

The fields keep their values: each one is saved in a hidden setting (CAMERA_CALIBRATION_...) as soon as it holds a valid value, so the panel opens with the last values used.

Preparing the Grid

  1. Press GENERATE GRID PDF. The grid is saved as calibration_grid.pdf in the project’s data folder, and GRID COLS and GRID ROWS are filled in with its size: 4 x 6 dots with the default values.

  2. Print it at 100% scale, not “fit to page”, on matte paper (glossy paper can reflect the light and hide dots).

  3. Check the spacing with a ruler. If it is not exactly the one requested, enter the real value in MEASURED SPACING (mm). Only the px/cm heatmap depends on it: the lens distortion does not depend on the size of the grid.

  4. Mount it flat on a rigid surface, such as a piece of cardboard or a board.

To cover a larger area, for example the whole floor of the box, build a bigger grid from several sheets: set MARGIN (mm) to 0, so that the dots on the left and bottom edges of each sheet are cut in half and are completed by the next sheet when the sheets overlap. Use the grey lines to align them, and enter the total number of dots of the assembled grid in GRID COLS and GRID ROWS.

Capturing Frames

  1. Hold the grid in front of the box camera, with all its dots visible, and press CAPTURE FRAME. The detected grid is drawn on the preview for 2 seconds, and then the preview goes back to the live image.

  2. Repeat with the grid at different positions, distances and angles. Cover the whole image, including the corners and edges, where the distortion is larger, and tilt the sheet in different directions.

  3. From the fourth frame on, each capture runs the calibration with all the frames captured so far, in the background. More frames give a more reliable result: 10 to 15 well distributed frames are a good target.

LOAD IMAGE uses an image file as a frame instead of the live camera, and CLEAR FRAMES discards all the frames to start over. All the frames of a calibration must have the same size. The frames are discarded when you leave the CALIBRATION screen; save the result before leaving.

If the grid is not detected:

  • Check that GRID COLS and GRID ROWS match your grid, and that all its dots are visible and in focus.

  • Leave SPACING (px) blank: a wrong value makes the detector reject the dots.

  • Avoid reflections and strong shadows on the sheet.

Top of the image

The frames include the status bar that the camera draws at the top of the image (about 1/12 of its height), so the dots under it are not detected. Keep the grid below it.

Reading the Results

After each calibration the panel shows:

  • Calib reproj error: the mean distance, in pixels, between the detected dots and where the calibrated model places them. Below 1 px is good; with good frames it is usually between 0.1 and 0.5 px. A high value usually means blurred or badly detected frames: clear them and capture new ones.

  • Distortion: the type and strength of the radial distortion, from k1. Negative is barrel distortion (edges bulging outwards, typical of wide-angle lenses), positive is pincushion.

  • k1, k2, p1, p2: the distortion coefficients. k are radial and p tangential (a lens not exactly parallel to the sensor); p are usually close to zero.

  • Principal pt offset: how far the optical centre is from the centre of the image.

Once there is a result, each new capture also shows Physical adjustment: the tilt (left/right and front/back) and the roll of the grid with respect to the camera, with hints such as “raise right”. The tilt is rated GOOD below 2° and OK below 5°, and the roll GOOD below 1° and OK below 3°; anything else is POOR. To check that the camera looks straight down at the floor of the box, lay the grid flat on the floor and adjust the camera until the tilt is GOOD.

The four plots on the right show:

  • Residual error: the reprojection error of every dot used. If it is concentrated in one area, capture more frames there.

  • Lens distortion field: how much, and in which direction, each part of the image moves when the distortion is corrected.

  • Tilt and roll: the shape of the grid in the last frame (red) compared with the same grid seen straight on (green).

  • px/cm: how many pixels a centimetre covers across the grid in the last frame.

Result

SAVE JSON saves the result as camera_calibration.json in data/<SYSTEM_NAME>/, next to the other calibrations of the system, replacing the previous one. Besides the lens model, it records everything needed to understand the calibration later, even if the panel’s values change afterwards:

Field

Description

date, system_name, village_version

When, on which system and with which version of Village it was made.

camera_matrix

[[fx, 0, cx], [0, fy, cy], [0, 0, 1]], in pixels: focal lengths and optical centre.

dist_coeffs

[k1, k2, p1, p2, k3]: the distortion coefficients, in OpenCV order.

The mean reprojection error, in pixels.

image_size_wh

Size of the calibrated frames. The calibration is only valid at this resolution.

n_images_used

Number of frames used.

spacing_mm, measured_spacing_mm, spacing_px

The grid and detector values (null when blank).

frames

Every frame used: its source (the box camera, with its resolution and lens position, or an image file), the grid size, the spacing and the number of dots found.

description

What each of these fields means.

Using the Calibration

The calibration is not applied automatically: the camera, the tracking and the videos keep working with the original image. Use it in your own analyses, for example to correct the positions of the animal saved in the CSV file of each video. The positions must come from a video recorded at the resolution in image_size_wh.

import json

import cv2
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

with open("data/my_system/camera_calibration.json") as f:  # data/<SYSTEM_NAME>/
    calibration = json.load(f)
K = np.array(calibration["camera_matrix"])
dist = np.array(calibration["dist_coeffs"])

frames = pd.read_csv("video.csv", sep=";")
tracked = frames[(frames["x_position"] >= 0) & (frames["y_position"] >= 0)]
points = tracked[["x_position", "y_position"]].to_numpy(np.float32)

# positions as they would be seen through a lens without distortion, in pixels
corrected = cv2.undistortPoints(points.reshape(-1, 1, 2), K, dist, P=K).reshape(-1, 2)

To correct whole images, use cv2.undistort(image, K, dist).
